Section II: Triangle Meshes and Half-Edge Data Structure

Part 3: Area-weighted vertex normals

To compute the area-weighted normal at a vertex, I traversed all incident triangles using the half-edge structure. For each non-boundary triangle, I computed the cross product of two of its edge vectors and added the result to an accumulated normal. Because the magnitude of this cross product is twice the triangle's area, larger triangles naturally contribute more weight. Finally, I normalized the accumulated vector to produce a unit vertex normal.

Part 4: Edge flip

I implemented the edge flip as a constant-time local operation on the two triangles adjacent to an interior edge. I first saved references to all six local half-edges, four vertices, five edges, and two faces before modifying any connectivity. I then reassigned each affected half-edge's next, twin, vertex, edge, and face pointers so that the shared edge connected the two previously opposite vertices. Finally, I updated the representative half-edge pointers for the affected vertices, edges, and faces. Boundary edges are returned unchanged, and the operation does not create or delete any mesh elements.

To avoid difficult pointer bugs, I labeled the complete local configuration before writing the assignments and reset every pointer in the two modified triangles, including pointers whose targets did not change. I tested the operation by flipping multiple edges on the teapot and checking that the triangulation changed locally without creating holes or disconnecting the mesh.

Part 5: Edge split

I implemented the edge split as a constant-time operation on the two triangles adjacent to an interior edge. The operation inserts a new vertex at the midpoint of the original edge and connects it to the two opposite vertices, replacing the original two triangles with four. I reused the original edge and two original faces, then allocated exactly one vertex, three edges, two faces, and six half-edges. After creating the new elements, I assigned every affected half-edge's next, twin, vertex, edge, and face pointers and updated the representative half-edge pointers for all local mesh elements. The new vertex points along one half of the original split edge, which will be important when the operation is used by Loop subdivision. Boundary edges are left unchanged because boundary splitting is not required for the base assignment.

The main challenge was keeping the expanded local connectivity consistent. I labeled every element before allocating or rewiring anything, drew the four resulting triangle cycles, and explicitly reset all pointers instead of only changing the ones that appeared different. I tested the result with repeated splits and with combinations of splits and flips. The mesh remained connected and did not develop holes or detached faces.
